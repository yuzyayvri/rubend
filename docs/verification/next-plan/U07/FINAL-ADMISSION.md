## A protocol chosen without release contents

`final-evaluation-protocol.mjs` describes the complete proposed experiment.
Its test checks 57,678 unique slot identities without loading cube contents.

| Population | Planned requests | Conditions |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Uniform release quality | 50,000 | All 10,000 release cases, five primary configurations, one-second warm requests. |
| Isolated timing | 6,400 | Thirty-two release IDs, ten configurations, five alternating cohorts, two duplicates, cold and warm clocks, one-second budget. |
| Longer budget | 192 | The same 32 IDs, six configurations, ten-second warm budget. |
| Structural cases | 1,086 | 181 predeclared IDs, six configurations, one-second warm budget. |

The timing IDs are the first 32 positions in a fixed SHA256 rank of integer case
IDs. Their choice does not inspect facelets or solver results. Quality uses four
matched CPU lanes under one four-GiB batch ceiling and does not support isolated
latency rankings. Timing uses one actor per fresh four-GiB CPU2 job. Every solver
uses one search thread. Optional U00 profiles and vcube 104 remain configuration
points, not additional implementations. Rubend's exact-request default is a
same-binary contract variant, separate from its fast contract.

The structural IDs reserve the existing 163 shallow BFS oracle cases, superflip,
half-turn and near-symmetric states, inverse word pairs and rotations. Their
actual source-bound artifact is still pending. An explicit justified optimum is
required for every numerical optimum gap. Unknown gaps stay null. Correlated
structural variants will not be treated as independent uniform samples.

Ten, hundred and thousand-millisecond checkpoints mean candidate arrival during
the same one-second request. They do not mean separately requested budgets or
ten-millisecond solver contracts. The separate longer-budget population supplies
the ten-second quality point. Cold-process timing does not mean cold disk cache.

Duplicate noise above five percent, CPU windows shorter than one second, missing
cohorts, coverage changes and failures keep comparative timing claims
inconclusive. A proposed speed improvement must exceed ten percent and pass the
predeclared uncertainty gates. The final statistics implementation is pending;
no speed claim is made by the protocol test. Completed noisy or failed slots
cannot be repeated for luck. Only never-executed slots may continue after a
bounded checkpoint.
